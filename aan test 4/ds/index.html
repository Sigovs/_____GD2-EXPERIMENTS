GD2 · Design System v1

Measured off the hero, not invented for it.

The hero is the only screen this brand has built, so it is the only evidence the system has. Every token is either a value the hero proved or is marked AUTHORED with the job it does.

Contrast figures are computed from the live tokens as the page loads, so the numbers follow the CSS rather than a note someone forgot to update. What they check is flat ink on flat ground. Type over photography, glass, blur or a gradient is not covered — those are solved per instance, against the worst region the run actually overlaps.

01

Ground and ink

One ground for the whole site — a second one is what produced the horizontal seam the hero had to be repaired for. The ink ladder is measured over it, and --ink-48 is the last step that can carry information.

02

Two accents. Two jobs.

Orange — the promise

What the visitor gets, and the control that reaches it. In the hero: the word know., the rule under the paragraph, the plus that opens the offer. Not annotation, not chrome.

Cyan — the instrument

Signal, data, annotation, focus, active state, chrome. In the hero: the numeral in the wordmark, every leader and anchor on the route, the readouts, the log-in edge. Not the promise.

Neither hue carries meaning on its own — each is also carried by position and by a label. The two solid values are shown as fills because that is how they are used; the alpha values are shown doing their job, because a wash rendered as a block is a colour this system does not contain.

03

Lines and surfaces

The escalation order is the whole anti-box policy: air → hairline → surface shift → a bordered box. The box is the fourth answer. None of these tokens is a colour — each is a line, an edge or a film, so each is shown doing that rather than filled into a square.

04

Type — two voices, seven ranks

A new role gets a size and a weight, not a new typeface. The mono earns its place because fixed-advance figures make a readout scan as a ledger — something the grotesque cannot do at any weight.

Seven ranks, countable down the left edge. Lead and Quiet label are variants — the same rank wearing a different size or ink — and they hang off the rank they belong to rather than claiming one of their own.

14px is the floor. Navigation, the phone number and the log-in label sat at 13 and 13.5px in the first build; all three carry information, so all three were raised. Below the floor, type is decoration — and decoration carries nothing.

05

Space — 4px base, one scale

Internal gaps are always smaller than the gap to the next block. That relationship is the grouping; a uniform gap deletes it.

Gutter --gut never falls below 24px. Page measure --col caps at 1560px, the masthead one step in at 1300px. Sections are bottom-heavy, roughly 1:1.3, at every width — reading runs downward, so a block leans into the space below it.

06

Primitives and components

Two levels, and the difference is whether the thing can be taken apart. A primitive is a mark — it has no smaller parts. A component is built from primitives and type, and owns a job on the page.

Adding to either level is a decision, not a reflex: name the job the existing set cannot do, and say it out loud. That is a bar to clear, not a door that is closed.

Primitives

.ico — icons

One set. A 12-unit box, 1px stroke, no fill, currentColor, sized to the cap height of the type beside it. An icon never carries meaning the label does not also carry.

07

Controls

Three ranks, one primary per view. Gradients are kept off controls deliberately: a flat surface gives each control one stable value to be read against, which keeps rank legible across the set and keeps the contrast check meaningful. Focus is a 2px cyan ring — try tabbing through this row.

08

Rows, and text over an image

Rows are separated by a hairline, not by a card each. Legibility over an image is fixed at the background layer, solved against the worst pixel the run actually overlaps — never by weighting the type, never with a text shadow.

10

The rules, in nine lines

Foundations, not commandments. Each one is here because the hero paid for it; each can be revised the same way — by evidence, in writing, with the reason recorded.

  1. Hierarchy is legible before a word is read. Two ranks are never near-identical.

  2. Every text/background pair is measured, and the ratio is stated. Text over an image is fixed at the background layer.

  3. Functional text is never below 14px, at any width.

  4. Every value resolves to a token. The exceptions are 1px hairlines, 0, 100%-class values, and drawn-mark geometry.

  5. Internal gaps are smaller than external. Space never collapses to zero.

  6. Orange is the promise; cyan is the instrument. Neither does the other's job.

  7. Air, then a hairline, then a surface shift, then a box. The box is the fourth answer.

  8. A shadow is a claim about elevation, and it must be true. Only genuinely floating layers cast one.

  9. Every animation has an authored still, and no route is discoverable by hover alone.

Contrast figures on this page are computed at load from the live token values, so a token that drops below its threshold turns orange here before it reaches a visitor.

What the check covers: one flat ink composited over one flat ground. What it does not: type over photography, over glass, behind blur, or on a gradient — where the value varies across the run and the only honest measurement is against the worst region it actually overlaps. Those are solved per instance, and the hero's scrim numbers are the record of one such solution.
